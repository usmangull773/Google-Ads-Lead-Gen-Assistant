What we found

Your brief describes a mature setup: tracking is in place, the manager owns strategy, and the accounts need someone to catch problems early. The real risk with several lead gen accounts is a rising CPL that nobody explains until the month is gone. Three things decide whether that happens.

Impact: High

A rising CPL has two possible causes, and they need opposite fixes

If demand moved, the right response is patience or a budget conversation. If the account broke, waiting costs money every day. Splitting CPL into its two parts shows which one moved.

1

Baseline every account in week one

Record spend, leads, CPL and target CPL for each account, then compare Google Ads leads with the CRM.

Why: you cannot judge a rising CPL without knowing where each account started.

2

Triage at the start of every window

Check spend pacing, leads and CPL against target, then rank the accounts that need attention today.

Why: the account bleeding money gets the first hour, not the last.

3

Clean search terms daily

Add negatives with the right match type, and promote converting search terms to keywords.

Why: in lead gen, search term quality is the fastest lever on conversion rate.

4

Refresh ads and assets in weeks two and three

Replace low rated RSA headlines, images, sitelinks, callouts and structured snippets, one test at a time.

Why: better ad relevance lifts Ad Rank and brings CPC down.

5

Check geo, device and audience in weeks three and four

Find out-of-area postcodes and weak device or demographic segments, and send adjustments to your manager.

Why: these are cheap leaks that grow quietly in local service accounts.
